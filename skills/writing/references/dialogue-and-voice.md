# Dialogue and voice

Voice fields are prose and roleplay context only. Do not produce audio or TTS instructions.

## Where the voice comes from

1. The character's `voice` block: description, tags, accent, dialect, and `instruct` if the author wrote manual guidance (follow it).
2. If accent or dialect is missing, inherit `regional_defaults` from the location, walking up the parent chain.
3. Then the facts: species (vocal range, what they can pronounce), race, age, class, faction culture, education and literacy, languages spoken, and the language of the conversation.

Express voice through diction, sentence length, rhythm, idiom, what they avoid saying, and the occasional dialect marker. Do not phonetically spell whole speeches; use two or three markers and word choice.

## Knowledge in speech

A character says only what they know and believe. Folk, official and true versions differ; give each character the version their traditions, faction and place hold. Misinformation sounds confident. A character who knows the truth may deflect, lie or stay silent for a reason.

## Language and names

- If characters speak different languages, say which language the scene is in, who is working in a second language (simpler, formal, accented), and who needs an interpreter. Intelligibility scores between related languages indicate how much is understood.
- Use naming conventions and honorifics of the speaker's culture. How someone addresses another shows rank and relationship.
- In-world oaths and idioms come from the world: its gods, animals, trades, climate, history. With profanity `invented-only`, use these, not modern curses.

## Anachronism check

No idioms, metaphors or units from technology or concepts that the flags or era forbid (no "clockwork" in a world without clocks, no "light speed" without science of it). Measure in in-world units.

## Style and dialogue

The prose style governs narration; dialogue has its own formality per character. A formal narrator can contain slangy speakers. Dialogue tags, stage business and interiority follow the style's internal monologue setting.

## Tabletop speech

When voicing an NPC for a GM: give a three-line speech sketch (rhythm, favourite phrase, tell), two sample lines, what they say when pressed, and what they never say. Keep their knowledge limits explicit.

## Checks

Does each speaker sound different? Does anyone know something they cannot? Does anything in the speech date the world wrongly? Does the exchange change a relationship, a plan or what someone knows? Record changes that matter (relationship, secret learned, promise made).
