# Distortion mechanisms

Pick one or two per tradition and name the cause. Record the mechanism in `distortions` and the cause in `distortion_causes`. Distortion is a result of people, institutions and language, never random noise.

| Mechanism | What happens | Typical cause | Suggested fidelity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Telescoping | A long process (decades of famine, a ten-year siege) is remembered as one night or one battle; or a distant event is remembered as recent. | Oral storage favours single scenes; no written chronology. | simplified |
| Hero fusion | Several leaders, inventors or saints merge into one figure who did everything. | A tradition needs a face; lineages or institutions claim the one name. | conflated |
| Moralising | The event becomes a lesson: the victims were proud, the disaster a punishment. | Priests, parents and elders use stories to teach rules (often the real rule beneath). | embellished |
| Propaganda by the victors | The winners write the record: the defeated become monsters, the conquest a liberation. | A regime or faction controlled the scribes, schools or songs. Needs a named faction and a motive. | inverted |
| Euphemism | A shameful or dangerous event is renamed ("the time the machines malfunctioned"). | Shame, fear of authorities, ritual avoidance of the true name. | simplified |
| Mistranslation | A word is misread when the story crosses a language, or a dead language's term is reinterpreted. | A language boundary or language shift; record `language` and which language changed. | garbled |
| Taboo-induced silence | People stop speaking of an event or place; only the prohibition survives, without the reason. | Trauma, punishment for speaking, a ritual ban. | garbled (status fading or suppressed) |
| Conflation of two events | Two similar disasters, wars or migrations merge into one account. | Similar place, similar consequence, a gap in the record between them. | conflated |
| Natural-feature etymology | A landscape feature or place name is explained by a story that grew up to explain the name, not the reverse. | A real name from a lost language, folk-explained in the current one. | invented (with a real kernel in the language) |

## Using them well

- Ask what the distortion is for. Moralising serves teachers, propaganda serves rulers, euphemism serves the ashamed, silence serves the frightened.
- Distortions compound with time but not evenly. A festival can preserve a date perfectly while its explanation is wholly invented.
- Keep the true kernel somewhere, usually in a detail nobody had a reason to change (a number, a place, a day, a name of a tool). That kernel is what a clever character can use.
- `invented` fidelity means no real event stands behind the details, only a real origin for the need to tell it. Still set `origin_events` to the event that created the need.
- `inverted` is for stories that say the opposite of what happened. It needs a clearly motivated source and usually a contradicting variant held by the other side.
