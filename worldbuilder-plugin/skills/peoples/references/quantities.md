# Quantities and arithmetic

Write each calculation in the entity prose or in your reply. Use the project's calendar and WorldDate system.

## Generations
- Mean generation length G is about 1.0 to 1.6 times `maturity_age` (parents typically have children from maturity to mid-life). For a species with lifespan L and maturity M, usually M <= G <= L/2.
- N generations take N x G years. Ten generations of a 25-year-maturity species is 250 to 400 years, not 100.
- Dynasties: a lineage founded Y years ago has about Y / G generations; the number of named rulers is up to Y / average reign (average reigns are 10 to 30 years when heirs inherit at death, shorter with violence, longer for long-lived species).
- A lineage with S known rulers over Y years needs average reign Y / S inside a plausible range for the species.

## Character ages
- birth year = reference year - age (state the reference date for the age).
- Parent age at child birth >= `maturity_age` (the old validator used 16 as an absolute floor; for most species use M) and <= fertile limit (about L x 0.6 for the mother in sexual species, unless stated otherwise).
- Siblings share parents; birth gaps follow the reproduction cycle (at least about 9 months for humans; use the species' gestation).
- A character is alive from birth to death: check every event, reign, marriage and journey date. They cannot hold office before M, and rarely before an age customary in their culture.
- Death must follow last appearances; `status: dead` characters cannot lead a faction or act after death.
- Generation overlap: grandparent-grandchild must be possible (two maturity spans).

## Population and carrying capacity
- Carrying capacity ~ productive area x yield per area / food needed per person. The food, yield, land-per-person, pastoral and fishing numbers (an adult human eats about 250 to 350 kg grain-equivalent a year) are owned by the `economy-and-trade` skill: see its heuristics, section 1. Use them; do not restate them here.
- Growth (owned here): sustained pre-industrial growth is about 0.1 to 1 percent a year; bursts after catastrophe up to 2 to 3 percent for a decade or two; above 3 percent is never sustained. Doubling time at 1 percent is about 70 years.
- Losses: famine, plague, war can take 10 to 40 percent; recovery takes decades for fast breeders and centuries for slow breeders.
- City sizes: 10,000 people needs a large rural hinterland or trade-fed food; 100,000 or more needs river or sea supply and a state.
- Armies: see the `economy-and-trade` skill, heuristics (standing force about 1 percent of population, 2 percent briefly, militia 5 to 10 percent for a season); logistics need food and transport from that skill.
- Total species population = sum of race populations; location populations should sum with the people's distribution.

## Language and migration time
- Dialect (owned here): 200 to 500 years separation; distinct languages: 1000 to 1500; family: 3000 or more (see `language-and-names.md`).
- Migration speed: on foot 10 to 30 km a day for a band, but a whole people migrates about 1 to 10 km a year over generations unless fleeing; consult travel rules in `geography-and-climate` and transport flags.

## Long-lived species and the accounting problem
A species with a lifespan of 500 or more years: fewer generations in recorded history, larger elder class, slow turnover. Explain how young people gain status, how heirs wait for inheritance, and how population stays below capacity (low fertility, long gaps between children, fertility tied to resources).
