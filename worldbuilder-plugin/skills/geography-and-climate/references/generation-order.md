# Generation order and rules of thumb

Use these numbers to check, not to constrain: a world may break any of them if it records a cause (engineering, different physics, or magic only if the gate allows) in the flags or the location. Unexplained breaks are errors.

## 1. Astronomy

| Quantity | Rule |
|---|---|
| Year length | years = sqrt(a^3 / M), a in AU, M in solar masses. Earth: 1 AU, 1 sun = 1 year. |
| Habitable zone | d from 0.75 x sqrt(L) to 1.15 x sqrt(L) AU (L in solar units). Sun: roughly 0.75 to 1.15 AU. |
| Axial tilt | 0 degrees: no seasons. 23 degrees: Earth-like. Above 45 degrees: extreme, poles hotter than equator in summer. |
| Tropics / polar circles | Tropics at +/- tilt; polar circles at +/- (90 minus tilt). Sun overhead only between the tropics. |
| Day length | Longer days mean larger day-night swings and stronger winds; very slow rotation (above ~ 10 Earth days) gives extreme interiors. |
| Tidal lock | Likely for planets in the habitable zone of small red dwarfs. Gives fixed day and night side; habitable band near the terminator; no latitude-based seasons. |
| Moons | Large moon: strong tides (wide intertidal zones, tidal rivers, ports timing by tide). No moon: weak tides, less stable tilt. Moon cycle should match the calendar. |
| Gravity | Surface gravity ~ density x radius. Heavier: flatter mountains, shorter, sturdier fauna, harder flight. Lighter: tall mountains, large flyers. |

## 2. Temperature

- Sea-level mean temperature falls from equator (~ 26 C) to poles (~ -20 C, or colder inland); about 0.5 to 0.7 C per degree of latitude beyond ~ 25 degrees.
- **Lapse rate:** about 6.5 C lower per 1000 m of altitude (dry air ~ 9.8, saturated ~ 5). Tree line at about 10 C warmest-month mean; permanent snow where the warmest month stays near 0 C.
  - Example: a plain at 15 C mean at sea level is ~ 8.5 C at 1000 m and ~ 2 C at 2000 m.
- **Continentality:** coasts have mild ranges (summer and winter within ~ 10-15 C of each other). Deep interiors on large continents swing 30 to 60 C between seasons. Distance from sea of ~ 1000 km or more counts as continental.
- **Ocean currents:** warm current coasts are 5 to 10 C warmer than their latitude suggests (Europe's west coast); cold current coasts are cooler and dry with fog (Atacama, Namib).
- Polar regions: snow does not melt, ice sheets build up if land is at the pole; sea ice at the pole otherwise.

## 3. Precipitation

- Equatorial belt (0 to 10 degrees): very wet (2000 to 4000 mm), convective daily rain. Subtropical belt (15 to 35): dry (below 500 mm), most deserts are here. Mid latitudes (40 to 60): moderate (500 to 1200 mm), storm tracks. Poles: very dry (cold deserts, below 250 mm).
- **Rain shadow:** moist air rising over a range drops rain on the windward side and descends dry on the lee. Windward slopes can get 2 to 5 times more precipitation than the lee; lee deserts extend tens to hundreds of kilometres. The wind direction (trade winds from the east in the tropics, westerlies at mid latitudes) decides which side is dry. No range, no rain shadow.
- **Distance from sea:** precipitation generally falls with distance inland unless a range or monsoon intervenes.
- **Monsoon:** a large landmass poleward of a warm ocean in the tropics or subtropics reverses winds seasonally: a wet season (summer, 60 to 90 percent of rain in 3 to 4 months) and dry season (winter). Needs a big continent and a big ocean, usually with a high plateau or range to the north.
- **Mediterranean** pattern: dry hot summers, wet mild winters, on west coasts at about 30 to 40 degrees. **Lee deserts** appear where westerlies cross a coastal range.
- Orographic uplift also makes mountains wetter and cloudier at medium altitude, then drier above ~ 3500 m.

## 4. Climate zones (check table)

| Zone | Typical mean annual temp | Rain | Growing season |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tropical rainforest | 24 to 28 C all year | above 2000 mm, no dry season | continuous |
| Tropical savanna / monsoon | 22 to 28 C | 800 to 1500 mm, strong dry season | 150 to 300 days |
| Hot desert | 20 to 30 C, huge day swings | under 250 mm | irrigated only |
| Semi-arid steppe | 8 to 20 C | 250 to 500 mm | 100 to 200 days |
| Mediterranean | 12 to 20 C | 400 to 800 mm, winter-wet | 250+ days |
| Humid temperate | 8 to 15 C | 700 to 1500 mm | 150 to 250 days |
| Continental | 0 to 8 C (summer 15 to 25, winter below -10) | 400 to 900 mm | 100 to 160 days |
| Subarctic (taiga) | -5 to 2 C | 300 to 600 mm | 50 to 100 days |
| Tundra | -15 to -5 C | under 300 mm | under 50 days |
| Ice cap | below -15 C | under 150 mm | 0 |
| Alpine | falls 6.5 C per 1000 m | high, much as snow | falls with altitude |

Growing season shortens roughly 7 to 10 days per 100 m altitude in temperate zones, and about 4 to 5 days per degree of latitude toward the poles. Anything under 90 frost-free days limits the crops to barley, rye, potatoes, turnips; under 60 days is marginal farming.

## 5. Hydrology

- Rivers: source (highlands, rain, snow or glacier melt) -> downhill -> sea, or an inland sink (salt lake) in a dry basin. A river keeps flowing through a dry region only if its source is wet elsewhere (Nile, Colorado).
- Tributaries merge; rivers do not split into separate seas. Splits happen only at deltas, alluvial fans and braided plains near the end.
- Size: river discharge grows with the catchment area; long, big rivers need big wet catchments.
- Lakes: have an inflow; either an outflow (fresh) or none (salt or alkaline). In cold climates rivers freeze for part of the year, which closes navigation but opens ice roads.
- Floodplains and deltas are fertile and heavily populated; seasonal floods follow snowmelt (spring) or monsoon (summer).
- Waterfalls and rapids mark the head of navigation and make river portage points: natural city sites.
- Coasts: ports need shelter (bays, estuaries); steep coasts with cliffs are poor harbours; river mouths silt up over centuries.

## 6. Biomes (temperature x rainfall)

| | under 250 mm | 250 to 750 | 750 to 1500 | above 1500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hot (above 20 C) | hot desert | savanna, thorn scrub | tropical seasonal forest | tropical rainforest |
| Warm temperate (10 to 20) | desert / semi-desert | grassland, steppe, mediterranean scrub | temperate deciduous or mixed forest | temperate rainforest, cloud forest |
| Cool (0 to 10) | cold desert | steppe, boreal scrub | mixed forest, boreal forest | boreal / wet forest |
| Cold (below 0) | polar desert | tundra | tundra / taiga | glacier |

Biomes grade into each other over 50 to 300 km (ecotones) unless a barrier cuts across. Wetlands form where drainage is poor. Altitude stacks biomes like latitude does: each 1000 m is roughly 700 to 1000 km poleward.

## 7. Flora and fauna

- Pick staple crops from the growing season and rain: wheat, barley, oats (cool temperate); rice (warm, wet or irrigated); maize, potatoes (varied, highlands); millet and sorghum (semi-arid); cassava, yams, banana (tropical).
- Trophic pyramid: each level up holds roughly one tenth of the biomass below. A large apex predator needs hundreds of km² per animal; big herbivore herds need open grassland or savanna.
- Domesticates must fit the land: draft animals and herds on grassland, fowl and pigs in settlements, reindeer-like animals in tundra.
- Island and isolated species are endemic and often have fewer large predators.
- Fantasy beasts: give them a niche (diet, range, habitat) like real animals; flying giants need a strong food source and updrafts (coasts, cliffs, mountains).
- Create a species entity (see `peoples`) for any creature or plant that appears in a plot, a trade good, a religion or a danger. Others stay as named list entries.

## 9. Carrying capacity (people per km², sustainable without imports)

| Biome | Hunter-gatherer | Pre-industrial farming | Industrial / modern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fertile river valley / delta | 1 to 3 | 100 to 400 | 300 to 1000+ |
| Temperate farmland | 0.1 to 0.5 | 30 to 100 | 100 to 400 |
| Mediterranean | 0.2 to 0.5 | 30 to 80 | 100 to 300 |
| Savanna / semi-arid | 0.05 to 0.2 | 5 to 30 | 20 to 100 |
| Tropical rainforest | 0.05 to 0.2 | 10 to 40 | 50 to 200 |
| Boreal forest / taiga | 0.01 to 0.05 | 1 to 5 | 2 to 20 |
| Mountains | 0.05 to 0.2 | 5 to 30 | 5 to 50 |
| Desert | under 0.05 | 0.5 to 5 (oases much more) | 1 to 100 with water imports |
| Tundra / polar | 0.01 to 0.05 | under 1 | under 1 |

Cities beyond local supply need a surplus region and a transport link: roughly 1 km² of cropped land supports 80 to 140 people at medieval yields (food numbers: the `economy-and-trade` skill, heuristics); a city of 100,000 needs the output of 300 to 1000 km² plus food moved over a feasible route (bulk food moves cheaply by water, expensively by cart: overland cart hauling is rarely worth it beyond ~ 100 km). Whole-planet totals: Earth-like land area ~ 150 million km² at pre-industrial density supports 0.5 to 1 billion people; industrial agriculture more than 8 billion. Check stated populations against this and record any food import or technological explanation.

## 10. Habitability

A location is habitable when it has water, food (or imports), tolerable temperature (about -30 to +45 C with shelter), breathable air and manageable hazards. Record which pieces are missing and how the inhabitants supply them (domes, magic, trade, adaptation). Hazards: tornado alleys, hurricanes (warm oceans 5 to 25 degrees latitude), blizzards, floods, volcanic and seismic zones along plate boundaries, wildfire in dry-summer forests, droughts in monsoon-dependent land.
