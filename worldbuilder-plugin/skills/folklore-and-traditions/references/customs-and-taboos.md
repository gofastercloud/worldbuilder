# Customs, taboos and the rest

The best customs are fossilised lessons. Work from cause to rule to ritual to forgotten reason.

## Five sources

| Source | Real cause | Typical tradition | Present-day `drives` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disease | An outbreak traced (wrongly or rightly) to water, food, an animal or a place | Washing rite, food taboo, quarantine custom, avoidance of a well | Boil or avoid the water; certain foods never shared; the sick isolated; effects on markets and diet |
| Diet and famine | A failed crop, a famine, a poisonous plant, a scarce animal | Fast days, harvest festival, ban on killing a breeding animal, proverb about seed grain | Festival dates follow the real harvest; livestock rules; grain never eaten before a day |
| Disaster | Flood, eruption, collapse, shipwreck | Taboo place, memorial day, "never build below the line" rule | Settlement avoids the valley; a route is detoured; building heights and sites |
| Trade | A route opening or closing, a monopoly, a swindle | Proverbs about prices, oaths over weights, guild rites | Contract formulae, who is trusted, which goods are exchanged ceremonially |
| War | A conquest, massacre, betrayal | Oaths, songs, banned names, victory festivals, shunning families | Who will not marry whom; flags and colours; forbidden words; day of mourning |

## Forms

- **Taboo**: a prohibition. Tie to geography (a place, a species) and name the behaviour it blocks. Record the consequence in the location and routes.
- **Festival and holy-day**: tie to the real calendar in `world/history/calendar.yaml`: the anniversary of the event, adjusted for any calendar change, which is itself a fertile source of discrepancy (a date drifting because the calendar was reformed).
- **Proverb**: tie to economics or a real incident ("sell before the ice", "a Keth promise"). The literal meaning shows the old cause.
- **Song and rhyme**: preserve number and sequence; use for the unreliable witness.
- **Oath**: names a witness (a place, an object, an ancestor) that traces to an event; breaking it has a social cost that you can model.
- **Naming habits**: children not given a dead hero's name, or all given it; place names that preserve a lost word.
- **Ritual**: an action preserved after its meaning has changed.

## Writing `drives`

Each `drives` entry should be something an observer could see: "No ships dock at the Saltmouth on the third day of the cold month"; "Merchants swear on a weighed stone"; "Roads around the Sunken Meadow are rerouted by two days". Avoid "people respect X". Then find the entity that should show the effect and edit it.

## Checking plausibility

- Does the custom make sense as a practical response to the cause, even if the explanation is not?
- Does it survive long enough: is it cheap to keep, tied to the calendar, or enforced?
- Would a neighbouring culture with a different history have a different custom? (If not, why not?)
